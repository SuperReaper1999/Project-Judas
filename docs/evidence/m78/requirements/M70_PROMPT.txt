## 3. Multi-target full-body IK

### Shared solve and explicit skeleton mapping

Extend the existing pose-composition infrastructure. Resolve a bounded set of
connected targets together, allowing shared pelvis/spine motion to reconcile them.
Several unrelated two-bone solves, with one more entity offset afterward, do not
satisfy this requirement. Choose the numerical method to fit Judas; document its
iteration bounds, joint model, supported topology and failure behaviour.

Use stable imported joint identities and explicit chain/body-root/spine mappings.
Names such as Hips, LeftFoot or Mixamo are not engine semantics. Preserve helper
nodes, multipart skin bindings and unmapped branches. Support the actual M66 full
multipart character or an approved equivalent without reducing its skeleton.
Include a differently named/proportioned mapping as proof against hidden rig rules.
This is same-skeleton posing, not cross-skeleton animation retargeting.

A target must support:
- Stable identity and enabled state; independent position/orientation influence.
- Position and quaternion orientation, including position-only/orientation-only use.
- An effector-local offset/frame: the sole or palm contact frame need not coincide
  with the imported joint origin or axes.
- Clearly documented coordinate spaces. Model-space and world-space targets are
  required; support-relative targets may be formed in JS from ordinary transforms.
- Bounded weights and a deterministic documented rule for competing constraints.

Permit a declared skeletal body root/pelvis to translate within authored limits and
allow the selected spine/ancestor joints to participate. That correction must come
from solving the target set, not a per-pose pelvis offset the game has to guess.
Other bone lengths remain intact; no silent limb stretching or arbitrary local translations to force success.
Respect validated joint ranges in declared local frames, including bend preferences
needed to avoid unstable straight/folded configurations. Do not invent universal
anatomical limits from names or assume every imported local axis has the same meaning.

### Pose correction is not character movement

IK modifies the instance's evaluated skeletal pose. A pelvis correction must NOT
move the entity, motor capsule, root-motion track, support frame, or dynamic body
behind the game's back. Keep authored source data and shared model resources immutable.
Start from the current reference pose; do not accumulate last frame's correction
as permanent drift. Any warm start must have explicit invalidation and bounds.

Preserve legacy layer composition, including carry and head-hiding layers. Do not
require inversion of a singular transform on an uninvolved hidden subtree. Diagnose
unsupported mapped transforms rather than silently changing scale/bind mathematics.
Define overlap with M65 limb IK explicitly; existing unrelated contributors keep
their ordering. A new solve is not permission to reorder every old layer globally.

Solved joint reads, attachments and skinning must agree for the same sample. Do not
write directly into GPU palettes or solve once per camera. A new world target must
not be reconstructed from whichever stale presentation matrix happens to be handy.
Support motion is sampled consistently: no one-frame disagreement between the board
frame used by the feet and the board/character frame drawn by the renderer.

Do not introduce implicit target feedback through sockets or the character's own
solved joints. Inputs are sampled at a defined boundary; cycles are rejected or have
explicit previous-sample semantics, never evaluation-order accidents.

### Numerical and coordinate behaviour

Reachable targets should converge within the documented bounded solve. Unreachable
or mutually inconsistent targets must give a finite bounded compromise and useful
per-target residual/status, not claim success, stretch bones or displace the entity.
Handle zero weights, missing keys, invalid quaternions, abrupt changes, nearly
straight limbs, rotation wraparound and target removal without NaNs or uncontrolled
flips. Declared damping/weight transitions must not secretly become gameplay timing.

Do not assume world Y is up. Any preferred up/bend/reference direction is explicitly
supplied or derived from the configured skeleton/target frame. Preserve existing
large-coordinate conventions: calculate small relative offsets before float solving.
This does not add origin rebasing, gravity policy or an automatic ground scanner.

## 4. Partial physical animation

### Drive a reference pose; do not overwrite simulation

Reuse Judas's bodies, ragdoll mappings, joints, collision solver and fixed-step
orchestration. Add the smallest reusable bounded pose-drive capability where the
existing joint system lacks it. No second physics world or replacement solver.

The target is a separate animation/IK REFERENCE pose. Selected bodies are driven
through physical forces/torques or appropriate constraint impulses. Their actual
pose comes from the physics solution, with ordinary contacts and inertia. Do not
teleport dynamic bodies onto the target, overwrite their velocity every step, or
render them at the target and call that physical response.

Resolve drive targets through the existing bone/body/COM offsets and joint frames.
An imported bone frame is not automatically the rigid body's frame or rest frame.
Use valid rotational errors and finite, timestep-aware control parameters. Expose
useful strength/stiffness, damping and effort limits with their units and meanings.
Targets beyond joint limits saturate/reconcile safely; no unbounded correction.
Keep contact/joint-limit enforcement authoritative over requested animation.

Control named/mapped regions or bounded joint sets with independent drive and
physical-contribution settings. State how overlaps resolve. Fade drive effort and
presentation blend separately where both exist: they are not interchangeable.
Zero drive means no active pose-restoring effort; ordinary constraints, material
response and authored passive resistance remain. Do not erase accepted rig tuning.
If an intermediate visual blend differs from the physical collision pose, document
that approximation and expose the actual physical result. It must not masquerade
as a body having reached its target or conceal failed collision response.

An active driven region must genuinely depart from its reference under an impact
and visibly reflect the simulated result. IK may help generate its target but must
not run afterward and overwrite dynamically owned joints. Unmapped children inherit
through the normal hierarchy once; avoid double-applying parent/entity motion.

### Motor-driven locomotion stays available

Provide a usable mode with the capsule CharacterMotor controlling locomotion and
selected regions physically responding. The rest of the pose can remain animated.
Use explicit animated/kinematic boundary anchors where needed. Document which
bodies collide and how the motor, mapped bodies and self-collision filters interact;
do not leave duplicate physical roots fighting over the character position.

A moving kinematic/motor anchor is an externally driven boundary, not proof of a
closed momentum-conserving system. Do not claim its reaction necessarily pushes the
motor. Between simulated dynamic bodies, retain the solver's normal reaction and
contact behaviour. No hidden root pinning or cancellation of incoming impulses in
fully dynamic mode merely to keep a demonstration standing.

For fully physical bails, allow the motor to relinquish locomotion authority through
an explicit handoff. Do not require a balance controller to use active joints while
falling: active characters can still lose support and fall. Generic gravity and
reference-frame handling remain Judas-owned, not hardcoded downward forces.

Retain ordinary contact/query participation and stable body-to-joint attribution.
Reuse existing events for bone-impact consumers; add only necessary generic identity
exposure if absent, not an injury/scoring subsystem or second collision-event bus.
Unchanged settled drive targets must not gratuitously invalidate sleep each frame;
meaningful drive changes/impacts must wake correctly. This is integration correctness,
not a reopening of every old ragdoll-settling/performance finding.

## 5. Authority transitions, timing and lifecycle

Expose explicit, reversible transitions among animation-only, selected physical
regions, full active ragdoll and passive ragdoll. Configuration/transition requests
validate before publication and apply at a safe simulation boundary.

On physical activation, initialize from the correct evaluated pose and motion:
- Account for root translation/rotation, bone-local motion, and the engine's support/
  frame conventions without adding a support velocity twice.
- Derive required new motion history from authoritative samples, not camera cadence.
  Reuse explicit caller-supplied motion when supported. Define first-sample/no-history
  behaviour honestly; do not invent a previous step or arbitrary launch velocity.
- Preserve existing body identity/state where possible and invalidate stale solver
  caches where necessary. No duplicate ragdolls, old collider ghosts or one-frame
  explosions during repeated toggles.

Active -> passive preserves current physical pose and velocities while removing
active drive. Resuming animation is a game request with explicit alignment/placement
and blend policy, using the existing motor placement/collision contract. Do not snap
through a wall, silently relocate the root, or launch an automatic get-up controller.
A refused transition retains a coherent previous state with a useful reason.

Sample drive references at fixed-step boundaries. Presentation interpolation may
smooth the output but cannot feed back into authoritative forces or change simulation
because another camera/render frame was evaluated. Specify the phase of script target
writes and resolved-joint reads; no promises that pre-physics reads see future results.

Keep new configuration/state inside normal prefab, scene, M67 named-authoring,
streaming and M61 save ownership. Save the declared targets, region/drive settings,
mode and transition state needed to resume; reuse existing physical-state capture.
Do not serialize pointers, GPU matrices or disposable solver caches. Restore through
fresh handles, reconstruct history safely, and do not replay activation impulses or
fresh-spawn defaults after load. Preserve compatibility deliberately; version real
format changes rather than rewriting old fingerprints to make checks pass.

Destroyed targets, skeleton reimport/replacement, region unload/adoption, project
switch and Play/Stop must invalidate the correct mappings and pending work. Use
existing qualified references and pin/retirement policies, not permanent stale pins
or per-frame VM construction. Define pending/not-ready behaviour for async models;
invalid or unresolved configurations must not leave half-created physical regions.
