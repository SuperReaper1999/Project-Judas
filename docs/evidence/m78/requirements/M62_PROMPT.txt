## 2. Shared infrastructure, distinct material models

Add a RuntimeWorld-owned deformable service integrated with the authoritative
fixed-step pipeline. Cloth and volumetric solids should reuse particle/state
storage, contact handling, attachment machinery, lifecycle and rendering bindings
where sensible. They need different internal constraints/material laws.

A practical representation separates:
- Shared immutable asset: simulation topology/rest geometry, masses or density
  inputs, constraint connectivity, boundary mesh, named attachment groups and
  render-to-simulation mapping.
- Per-instance simulation: positions, velocities, material/constraint state,
  attachments, contacts, enabled/sleep state and any plastic rest-state changes.
- Presentation: previous/current samples, deformed render buffers and bounds.

Do not represent every node as a full Judas Entity/RigidBody. One deformable owns
many simulation nodes. Use existing safe entity/asset identities and bounded
node/element references. Sharing an asset must never share mutable deformation.

Use a compact CPU-authoritative implementation first, with no new GPU-compute or
OpenGL-version requirement. Reuse JobSystem and resource loading if needed; workers
must not race PhysicsWorld, QuickJS or Renderer. No compulsory deformable update or
large allocation in scenes without deformables.

## 3. Numerical direction and a small first proof

Design recommendation for this brief: use XPBD/compliant constraints, with bounded
substeps and clear material parameters, as the shared starting point. For solid
elasticity, a tetrahedral strain-energy model with distinct distortional and
volumetric response is appropriate. References R1–R3 below support that direction;
they are not instructions to import another complete physics engine.

A better-fitting mature method is acceptable if you explain the concrete benefit
and retain these contracts. Pin and record any added dependency and its license.
Do not write a custom general tetrahedralizer or undertake a solver survey.

First establish a small cloth patch and a small elastic solid, then contact with
one static and one dynamic body. Prove those mechanics before building a park full
of examples. After repeated failure of the same approach, report the mechanism
and proposed change instead of endlessly loosening tolerances.

With XPBD, use the actual substep dt in compliance and damping. Define multiplier
initialization/accumulation/warm-start lifetime explicitly. Reconstruct velocity
consistently with corrected positions; do not apply the same reaction twice as
both a projection and a later impulse. Finite iteration error still exists: do
not advertise perfect timestep/iteration independence.

Position projection inside a documented constraint solve is legitimate. A demo
script teleporting vertices back into shape after the solve is not.

Keep the existing fixed-step cadence. Configure bounded deformable substeps and
iterations instead of slowing the whole engine or hiding instability with huge
unconditional velocity damping. Validate masses, elements and material ranges;
report invalid/nonfinite solves honestly rather than silently resetting rest pose.

## 4. Cloth and thin-surface capability

Support indexed triangle simulation meshes, not only a hardcoded rectangular
flag. Required mechanics:
- Mass distribution from authored surface density or explicit weights.
- In-plane stretch and shear resistance, separately controllable from bending.
- Bending resistance, explicit damping, gravity and external forces/impulses.
- Configurable contact thickness and friction, distinct from render thickness.
- Fixed pins, moving attachments and released/free nodes.
- Usable self-contact for folding fabric; external collision is not a substitute.

Provide a rectangular-sheet authoring helper and a reusable import path for
triangulated cloth meshes. Keep render UV seams/hard-normal splits from becoming
unintended physical tears. Welding must be intentional and topology-aware; do not
join separate layers simply because vertices happen to overlap.

For garment use, support a simple weight/mask or attachment-group workflow: e.g.
a cape's shoulders follow animated targets while the rest is simulated. A bounded
skin-reference tether is acceptable when explicitly authored, not a hidden force
pinning every vertex to its animation. No assumption of humanoid bone names.

Provide simple optional air-velocity/drag forcing so a flag responds to relative
airflow. An authored uniform airflow is sufficient; no weather or atmosphere
simulation rewrite, and no sine-wave vertex animation masquerading as wind physics.

## 5. Volumetric elastic and plastic solids

Support a reusable tetrahedral or equally justified volumetric simulation asset.
The minimum production route may import pre-tetrahedralized meshes and generate
simple block/beam/lattice fixtures. It must not require engine code changes for
every new object. Validate connectivity, orientation, degenerate elements and
boundary extraction. Preserve deliberate disconnected components.

The solid must resist both shape distortion/shear and volume change. A surface
balloon with only one total-volume constraint is NOT sufficient for this half of
M62. Demonstrate compression, bending and twisting, not just a jiggling sphere.

Expose mass/density, elastic response and damping with documented units and
meaning. Compliance parameters are acceptable; do not label an arbitrary slider
as a measured Young's modulus. Near-incompressibility is a controlled approximation,
not permission to normalize all vertices globally each frame. Monitor element
volume/inversion and give the supported deformation envelope honestly.

Include a modest optional irreversible/plastic response:
- Below an authored yield threshold, the object recovers elastically.
- Above it, a bounded update changes internal rest/plastic state.
- Remove the load: part of the deformation remains without ongoing forces.
- A second identical instance remains unaffected.

Use a material/strain-based rule, not an object-name dent, morph animation or
scripted rest-pose swap. Document whether plastic flow preserves rest volume or
models compressibility; update dependent rest data coherently. This is a modest
game-oriented material model, not calibrated steel/concrete, granular flow or
arbitrarily large plastic folding. Use an established formulation as appropriate;
R5 is an alternative-method reference, not a demand for another solver.

Keep topology fixed in M62. No tearing, cracks, bond failure, remeshing, separated
chunks or conversion into rubble. Those are the newly renumbered M63.

## 6. Contacts and actual rigid/deformable interaction

Use Judas's existing PhysicsWorld geometry, broadphase/filter vocabulary and safe
body handles. Establish a compact support matrix before integration: deformable
versus existing box/sphere/compound/terrain geometry, self-contact, other deformables,
CharacterMotor queries, raycasts and M42 events. Do not claim unsupported pairs.

Required: cloth and soft solids collide with ordinary static and dynamic primitive
bodies; representative cloth/soft-body contact works; self-folding cloth resists
passing through itself in the demonstrated speed/resolution envelope.

Do not rely solely on separated particle centres to claim a collision-free triangle
surface. Handle relevant vertex/face and edge/edge proximity or a justified bounded
surface-aware alternative. Use adjacency exclusions and accelerated candidates,
not all-pairs scans. Account for body point velocity in moving contact/friction.
A discrete/substepped approximation is acceptable if its limits are explicit and
ordinary curtain/cape/squeeze tests do not repeatedly tunnel. R4 explains why
surface contact must be considered separately from internal material constraints.

Two-way dynamic interaction is required in a modest proof: a body strikes/loads
cloth or a soft block, and both respond with finite mass/inertia. Do not treat all
bodies as immovable colliders and call it two-way coupling. Share reactions with
the normal rigid step through one defined coupling boundary; do not advance the
whole PhysicsWorld twice or apply independent contradictory responses afterward.

An animated or static attachment may be prescribed/infinite-mass support. Label
that distinctly from a dynamic rigid attachment. External work from prescribed
motion is not a closed-system conservation failure. Demonstrate reaction on a
free dynamic support and no unexplained energy growth in a simple unforced test.

Use M39 filtering. Provide ray picking of the current deformed surface and a safe
hit/location representation for applying a physical impulse or temporary attachment.
Reuse normal M44 dispatch where possible; make any separate query coverage explicit.
Never pretend an undeformed rest hull is the current exact collision surface.

Do not smuggle a general static-mesh collider, complete shape-cast expansion or
CharacterMotor redesign into M62. Deformable-local surface tests needed here are
not automatically a new general world-collision capability.

## 7. Attachments, animation timing and spatial authority

Support stable named node groups or weighted surface locations attached to:
- A fixed authored world anchor.
- A rigid body's local frame.
- A skeleton joint's local frame, resolved through existing pose infrastructure.

Attachment location/motion comes from the actual transform/pose, not clip names,
fade fractions or handwritten character dimensions. Resolve targets at the fixed
simulation boundary, with coherent target velocity/substep sampling. Do not drive
physics from interpolated render transforms. Rendering later interpolates the
simulated result consistently with the rest of Judas.

Inspect the current pose-evaluation order. Animation is one pose producer; passive
ragdoll is another. Deformables consume the chosen authoritative target without
owning the skeleton. No second animation evaluation with independent clocks.
No IK, balance or active-ragdoll implementation is required.

A minimal safe attachment/bone-reference facility is authorized if genuinely
necessary for cloth. That is not blanket authorization for the skate audit's
entire socket/IK wishlist. Generic public additions need normal documentation.

Define the entity/root policy for freely moving deformables. Do not simulate nodes
in world space and then apply root motion to them a second time. Gravity comes
through Judas's selected field at the relevant positions, including radial and
oblique cases; zero gravity is valid for this particle-based representation.
Respect fixed-origin double absolute/local coordinates. No live origin rebasing.

Disabling, releasing or destroying an attachment must have a documented physical
and lifetime result. Releasing an anchor is allowed; automatic stress-based
breakage remains M63. Explicit teleports/reset operations must coherently update
velocities, previous poses, contacts and bounds rather than imparting accidental
infinite anchor speed.

## 8. Rendering and material composition

A one-to-one path may be the simplest case, but prove at least one denser render
mesh follows a coarser simulation mesh correctly.

Keep deformed positions, normals/tangent handling and bounds coherent. Preserve
UV/material seams, M57 material slots/overrides, shadows, M33 secondary cameras,
M39 render masks and M37 culling. A cloth material may be two-sided through the

Use previous/current simulation state for smooth presentation. Do not run cloth
once per camera, and do not freeze it merely because the main camera looks away.
The shadow and visible mesh must describe the same presented deformation.

## 10. Streaming and persistence are part of delivery

Extend the actual M61 participant mechanism rather than inventing another save
format. Capture sufficient authoritative state to resume: node positions/velocities,
plastic/internal rest state, changed material settings, attachment state/references,
enabled/sleep state, and any solver history actually needed for stable continuation.
Assets/topology use stable identities and content compatibility. GPU buffers,
broadphase structures and derived normals are rebuilt, not serialized as pointers.

Required cold-process proof: save a moving cloth and a permanently deformed solid;
exit; reload in a new process. The cloth must not reset flat, the dent must remain,
and references must resolve to fresh safe handles without a first-step explosion.
Define cache reconstruction and compare bounded continuation, not an unsupported
promise of indefinitely bit-identical trajectories.

Use M59 region ownership and dependency rules. Independent state must suspend and
resume through the supported M61/M59 path. An attachment to a live body/character
in another region needs an explicit dependency/pin/adoption policy. Never unload
its target and leave a dangling attachment, or report a pinned region as unloaded.
Do not capture unbounded particle arrays repeatedly in every unload-budget slice.

Missing support must fail diagnostically, not silently omit a required deformable
from a successful save. Preserve authored reload versus restoring a saved runtime
state as distinct operations. Stop/project change cleans all simulation records.

## 13. Focused correctness checks

Keep a compact suite with independent simple expectations, then actual-world tests:

- Topology/mass: invalid assets rejected; total mass matches geometry/density;
  shared asset instances remain independent; render seams do not disconnect cloth.
- Material: known compliant extension or small-strain reference; cloth stretch/
  shear/bend respond separately; solid resists volume change AND shear; below-yield
  recovery versus retained above-yield deformation. Rest rotation is not strain.
- Numerics: compare two practical timestep/substep/iteration settings and report
  residual/damping changes; no uncontrolled energy growth in an unforced fixture;
  finite inversion/failure handling within the stated envelope.
- Contacts: moving box/sphere, friction relative to support motion, cloth fold
  self-contact, cloth/soft interaction, and dynamic rigid reaction. Include a
  surface-crossing case that particle-centre separation alone would not prove.
- Frames/attachments: rotated equivalence, radial/zero gravity, fixed-origin large
  absolute offset, animated bone target, dynamic support, release/target destruction
  and stale handle/slot reuse. No double-applied root or carrier motion.
- Presentation: denser render mapping, coherent normals/shadows/culling, two cameras,
  interpolation; presentation must not mutate authoritative simulation.
- Lifecycle: sleep/wake, runtime prefab, safe disable/reset, region unload/revisit,
  old-handle invalidation and a genuine cold-process save/load with retained dent.
- Public path: real VM cookbook/control execution, synced types/inventory, actual
  editor authoring/Play–Stop and moved exported project with required assets/notices.

Do not count a source scan or setter round-trip as proof of physical behaviour.
Do not manufacture failure evidence; preserve genuine defects and narrow follow-ups.

## Reference starting points — not an additional research assignment

These references inform the proposed implementation direction, not the skate
report's findings. Read the relevant method/limitations, not every cited paper.

R1. Macklin, Müller, Chentanez (2016), XPBD: Position-Based Simulation of Compliant
Constrained Dynamics. Compliance/constraint-force formulation and finite-iteration
limitations. https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf

R2. Macklin et al. (2019), Small Steps in Physics Simulation. Compare bounded
substepping with iterations; collision reuse has caveats and must fit this scene.
https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/smallsteps.pdf

R3. Macklin and Müller (2021), A Constraint-based Formulation of Stable Neo-Hookean
Materials. Distortional versus volume response for tetrahedral elastic solids;
not a complete plasticity, collision or game-engine integration implementation.
https://doi.org/10.1145/3487983.3488289

R4. Bridson, Fedkiw, Anderson (2002), Robust Treatment of Collisions, Contact and
Friction for Cloth Animation. Surface contact/thickness and render-subdivision
pitfalls; reference rather than a mandate for the entire offline algorithm.
https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/cloth-sig02/

R5. Müller et al. (2005), Meshless Deformations Based on Shape Matching. Alternative
game-oriented elastic/plastic representation; not calibrated structural mechanics
or authorization for topology change. Do not add a second solver just to use it.
https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/MeshlessDeformations_SIG05.pdf
