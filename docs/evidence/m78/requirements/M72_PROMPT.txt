## 1. Deliver the agreed capabilities

Claude's city currently approximates night with exposure/sky colour because its
scripts cannot control the sun, and hides smashed objects by parking them out of
sight. Establish the current implementation, then close those generic engine gaps.

Required:
- Runtime directional/sun light direction, intensity, colour and enabled state.
- Script-controlled environment settings sufficient for a real lighting transition,
  not merely darkening the final image. Reuse the existing environment representation.
- Entity-level and individual render-component visibility, independent of physics.
- Instance-local material properties, colour, opacity and texture overrides without
  modifying shared source assets or unintentionally changing other instances.
- Practical editor authoring and normal prefab/save/stream/export integration.

Judas supplies lighting, environment and rendering primitives. JS decides the time,
sun path, day length, which objects disappear and what a material change means.
Do not add native day/night gameplay, security logic or a hardcoded world clock.

Conner permits renderer restructuring or replacement where justified. Preserve
supported behaviour, not historical internals: do not build awkward workarounds
solely to avoid changing Renderer. Equally, permission is not a rewrite mandate.
Judas must own the implementation; do not substitute a third-party renderer or
material framework. Existing foundation dependencies need not be purged. New external
implementation dependencies require explicit agreement, not automatic adoption.

Not included: asset-pack distribution, retargeting, joint-subtree hiding/scaling,
weather/astronomy, new atmospheric scattering, GI/reflection research, a shader-graph
product, unlimited light counts or a general streaming/performance overhaul.

IMPORTANT: secondary cameras, picture-in-picture and in-world camera screens may
retain their CURRENT LOWER-FIDELITY rendering. Main-camera quality/feature parity
is explicitly NOT an M72 requirement. See section 6.

## 3. Runtime lighting and environment

Expose the existing sun/directional-light model through normal JudasJS and authoring.
A project can enable/disable it and change direction, intensity and colour at runtime.
Define direction space and whether it means light travel or direction toward the
source. Validate finite/nonzero directions; document colour space, intensity units
and supported ranges rather than silently changing existing lighting conventions.

Actual direct lighting and its supported shadows must follow these settings together.
Changing exposure alone does not satisfy this. Moving the sun must update shadow
orientation/invalidation without stale shadows, scene reloads or recompiling shaders
on every direction change. Disabling a light removes its direct contribution; it
need not make the scene black when ambient/environment illumination remains.

Expose the relevant existing sky/background and ambient/environment contribution
controls so scripts can coordinate them with the direct light. A bounded environment
strength control is sufficient where elaborate environment-map blending does not
exist; do not turn this into new sky or GI technology. Existing exposure remains an
independent presentation control, not a substitute for lighting. Inspect point/spot
controls and reuse them where available; document remaining limits instead of
promising a new unbounded lighting system.

Environment ownership must be explicit within the shared RuntimeWorld. Loading an
additive region must not accidentally reset the sun because it has different scene
defaults. Use the existing owner/override rules where sound; define replacement,
removal and multiple-writer behaviour without introducing a new arbitration framework.

The engine must not assume a universal world-up or a compulsory sun path. JS can
provide a direction directly or animate it around a chosen frame. No gravity/orbital
policy changes, automatic planetary lighting or origin rebasing are required.

## 4. Render visibility without changing the simulation

Provide a render-only entity switch and independently addressable render-component
visibility. Define how they combine: hiding the entity suppresses its renderables;
showing it again must preserve intentionally hidden component settings. Define child
scope explicitly rather than silently deactivating an entire hierarchy.

Cover ordinary primitives and imported static/skinned renderables through their
existing component/part identities. Preserve physics, queries, scripts, animation
needed by joint reads/sockets, audio and interaction state. Do not implement hiding
through movement, zero scale, collider removal, despawning or entity deactivation.

Visibility must affect applicable visible/depth/shadow submissions coherently; no
stale shadow or occlusion contribution from an ordinarily hidden renderer. Document
any separate existing shadow-only policy rather than introducing one accidentally.
Editor hierarchy/inspector access must still allow a user to find and restore hidden
objects; do not confuse editor overlays with runtime visibility.

Respect renderer-managed batching/instancing: changing one logical renderable must
not hide unrelated members or leave a stale baked submission. Geometry already
combined into one authored mesh is only independently addressable at its actual
supported part/component granularity; do not invent the original lost entities.

## 5. Material instances, including real opacity and texture changes

Use immutable shared material/model assets with bounded per-instance overrides.
Support the current shader's material parameters, including base colour, usable
opacity and the existing PBR/emissive properties where supported. Texture changes
must use normal asset references and existing supported material texture slots.
No raw shader editing or automatic new shading model is needed.

A whole-instance override and explicit material-slot/part targeting must fit existing
renderable bindings, including imported multipart models. Use stable identities and
useful diagnostics; do not make engine semantics depend on a generated mesh name.
Two entities sharing a model/material/texture remain independent unless the caller
explicitly elects an existing shared-edit operation. Clearing an override returns to
the correct authored/shared value. Source assets, import recipes and base materials
must not be rewritten by runtime calls.

Opacity must visibly work in the MAIN render path, not merely store an alpha value
that opaque shaders ignore. Expose/define opaque, cutout and alpha-blended behaviour,
including cutoff, colour/texture alpha interaction and depth/blend handling. Use a
bounded appropriate transparency strategy and state its ordering limitations. Prove
simple overlapping non-intersecting surfaces; arbitrary intersecting transparency,
refraction and physically coloured transparent shadows are not required. Document
shadow policy for alpha modes and prevent stale state when modes change.

Retain the current texture colour/data conventions, normal/tangent handling and
skinned/static paths. Reuse loaded immutable GPU resources; do not copy models,
reimport textures or compile shader programs for every colour/opacity update.
Texture requests can be asynchronous. Define pending/failed replacement behaviour,
preserve a coherent last-valid or documented fallback rendering, and reject stale
publication after a newer request, reset, destruction or region unload.

Batch material updates validate before publication; invalid input cannot leave half
an override applied. Expose useful status and reset/readback without returning mutable
renderer internals. Finite values, unsupported slots, missing assets and invalid
handles need actionable, bounded diagnostics rather than a per-frame error flood.

## 6. Keep secondary-camera rendering deliberately cheaper

Preserve the existing lower-fidelity render-to-texture path, cadence, target ownership,
feedback protection and resource safety. Do NOT upgrade it to full main-view lighting,
shadows, transparency or other expensive effects simply for feature parity.

Separate scene state correctness from rendering quality. Explicit render visibility
still governs what is submitted, and supported material/environment controls use a
coherent snapshot. Effects the auxiliary path does not support may retain their
existing simplified/omitted treatment; document that matrix instead of secretly
promising identical output. Main-view acceptance must not compare its pixels against
a simplified camera screen or require the screen to match its shadows/transparency.

Test a live camera texture on an in-world screen while changing supported state,
hiding/showing its source objects and retiring/reloading resources. Ensure no stale
handles, render feedback or shared GPU-state leakage corrupts the main view.
