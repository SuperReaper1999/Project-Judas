GOAL
An ordinary authored object or structure can carry load, deform where configured,
fail locally, separate into physical pieces and continue interacting with the world.
The fracture pattern is bounded by authored/baked topology; the actual sequence of
failure must respond to physical loading, not a prerecorded collapse animation.

Judas owns material failure, connectivity, safe topology changes and physical state.
Projects own fracture assets/material choices. JavaScript owns weapons, scoring,
destruction objectives, sounds and other game meaning.

## 2. Bounded fracture representation, not unlimited mesh destruction

Recommended starting direction: precomputed physical cells/chunks with explicit
interfaces/bonds, using M62 deformation and a local material-failure criterion.
A partition of the existing volumetric elements is sufficient. Cracks may follow
those interfaces; arbitrary runtime crack cutting/remeshing is NOT required.

Separate immutable source data from per-instance state:
- Asset: physical partition, rest mass/geometry, interface area/normal/centroid,
  stable part/interface keys, material assignments, render/interior-face bindings,
  supported collision representations and authored support/attachment locations.
- Instance: surviving interfaces, irreversible failure state, current connectivity,
  deformed/plastic state, physical fragment ownership and live attachments.
- Presentation: visible boundary faces, mapped vertices, normals, bounds and buffers.

Support at least one reusable imported/prepartitioned volumetric asset route and
small block/beam/panel generation helpers. Reject overlapping or invalid material
partitions. Render UV seams are not physical cracks; separate pieces must not weld
merely because their vertices coincide. Shared assets never share fracture state.

A cooked chunk/bond graph is established prior art [R1]. Its topology machinery
alone does not prove stress-driven collapse. Reuse mature geometry/bookkeeping code
where useful; do not write a general-purpose Boolean/Voronoi/tetrahedralization
package. Any added library must be pinned, licensed and integrated behind Judas
ownership. No replacement PhysicsWorld, PhysX runtime or GPU requirement.

Use ONE coherent topology/fragment-ownership model. It may support deformable pieces
and rigid descendants; it must not become two unrelated destruction systems whose
only common feature is a demo button.

## 3. Failure must come from physical demand

Implement local, irreversible material/interface failure using a demonstrated
stress, strain, traction or fracture-energy approximation appropriate to M62.
At minimum distinguish tensile opening and shear demand; define compression's role
rather than treating every absolute force as equivalent damage.

Failure strength is separate from elastic stiffness and plastic yield. A yielding
beam may retain a bend without necessarily breaking. Expose documented physical or
normalized engineering parameters; do not label an arbitrary number as calibrated
concrete toughness. This is game-oriented mechanics, not certified engineering.

Use the actual solved material/constraint state. XPBD force estimation, if M62 uses
it, requires the accumulated multiplier, constraint gradient and correct substep
units [R2]. Do not sum absolute solver-iteration corrections as independent impacts.
Do not accumulate gravity damage once per render frame or mistake rigid rotation
for strain. Any gradual damage law must use physical elapsed time; a simple brittle
threshold is acceptable when its scope is explicit.

Observe contacts, external loads and support reactions through the authoritative
solver. Apply a hit impulse ONCE. Do not also add a guessed equivalent explosion
or charge the same impact again through every contact callback.

A public explicit break/cut request may be useful for tools and gameplay, but must
remain visibly distinct from physically detected failure. Acceptance includes
breakage with NO script calling break and no object health counter driving it.

First prove a small loaded specimen: below threshold it survives; above threshold
an interface fails; remaining material remains intact. Compare two practical
substep/iteration configurations and report sensitivity, not perfect invariance.

## 4. Structural support and redistribution

A connectivity graph identifies attached pieces; it does not calculate whether a
connection can carry a load. Reuse M62 internal/cohesive constraints or a compact,
justified load model so changing a support changes demand in the remaining structure.
Stress-graph methods are another reference for this distinction [R3], not a mandate
to run a second competing solver beside M62.

Provide explicit supports in world or rigid-body local frames. An authored fixed
support is an external reaction source; a dynamic support must receive the existing
physical reaction. No global "bottom row" or object-name support detection.

Required demonstrations:
- An intact supported assembly holds a modest load.
- Removing/breaking one support redistributes load; remaining connections either
  hold or fail according to their capacity, not a hardcoded neighbour cascade.
- A detached connected component falls/moves as a component, rather than every
  internal element becoming a separate particle simply because it lost a support.
- A free assembly under uniform gravity does not acquire fictitious self-weight
  stress merely because it is falling. Supported and freely falling are different.

Use actual selected Judas gravity at the relevant positions. Prove rotated uniform
gravity and a small radial fixture. Respect local/reference-frame coordinates and
M23's fixed double absolute origin; no live origin rebasing or world-Y shortcut.

## 5. Real topology separation and progressive breakage

Failed interfaces must cease carrying tensile/shear connection through every
relevant constraint. Shared nodes, shape constraints, render bindings or attachment
indices must not secretly keep disconnected pieces welded.

Compute surviving connected components in stable order. If an interface opens but
an alternate path still connects the pieces, preserve that remaining load path.
Further loading may break other interfaces in a surviving fragment. The cooked
partition sets the finite fracture resolution; infinite recursive subdivision is
not required.

When a split requires duplicate interface vertices, partition their mass from their
incident material elements. Do not copy the complete original nodal mass into every
fragment. Do not delete tetrahedra merely to draw a crack. Expose newly internal
faces as boundary faces while retaining material, rest/plastic state and UV mapping.

At least one M62 yielding/deformable specimen must actually separate from its CURRENT
deformed state. Swapping it for pristine pre-broken copies does not meet this goal.
Continuing soft pieces retain their M62 solver; ordinary rigid fragments are also
required for a supported stiff/rigid specimen. These are two outcomes of the same
fracture ownership path, not two mandatory full constitutive solvers.

## 6. Atomic publication and safe fixed-step lifetime

Record failure candidates during the authoritative step; commit topology/body changes
at a documented safe boundary, never while iterating a solver array or invoking JS.
Deduplicate break requests by stable interface identity and topology revision.

Prepare and validate all child state before publication. The world must never expose
an old solid collider plus its new fragment colliders for the same material, or a
half-installed fracture where render, physics and queries disagree. Budget/capacity
failure retains the last coherent representation and reports/defer-retries safely.
It must not partially destroy an object and drop the pieces that did not fit.

At commit, update the affected constraints, attachments, contacts/broadphase, query
mapping, render bindings, bounds and resource ownership. Invalidate or remap caches
selectively; do not flush unrelated world joints or restart the entire PhysicsWorld.

Use immutable worker preparation where helpful. Only the authoritative thread may
publish entities, physical bodies and JS-visible results. Save/load, region unload,
script destruction and cancellation must not race a half-finished split.

## 7. Fragment mass, inertia and inherited motion

Every material cell has exactly one physical owner before and after a split.
Preserve total mass and use its actual mass distribution for centres and inertia.
Keep physical density/material data separate from M57 visual material properties.

For a rigid parent split without extra forces, each child inherits the parent's
angular velocity and point velocity at its own centre of mass:

v_child = v_parent + omega_parent cross (COM_child - COM_parent)

Do not give every child only the parent's linear velocity, zero all motion, or
apply the triggering impact to every child again. A static supported part has finite
authored material mass even though its support prevents motion before release.

For a deforming source, transfer the current element/node motion. If a sufficiently
stiff fragment is converted to a rigid body, derive linear and angular momentum
from that motion using the actual mass model and a consistent inertia. State any
nonrigid-energy loss from rigidification; do not add energy to make rubble dramatic.
A substantially deforming piece stays deformable rather than snapping to rest shape.

Test topology conversion separately from the external load: an unforced translating/
rotating split should preserve mass, centre of mass, linear and angular momentum
within declared numerical/proxy tolerances. Compression, fracture dissipation and
external support work are not reasons to claim exact total-energy conservation.

Collision proxy volume need not define the material mass. Declare approximation
bounds and reject invalid/singular fragment configurations, not arbitrary fake mass.

## 8. Collisions, query truth and attached objects

Reuse actual M62 contacts and ordinary PhysicsWorld collision primitives. Establish
a compact support matrix for intact structures, deformable fragments and rigid
fragments against rigid bodies, CharacterMotor, M44 queries and M42 events.

The supplied baseline may still lack general triangle-mesh/convex world colliders.
Do not silently assume they exist. Prebaked box/compound proxies or existing M62
surface contacts may serve supported assets. Prove at least one irregular or
multi-cell component with useful collision, not only perfect cubes. A tiny general
extension needed for those pieces is allowed; a full mesh-collision milestone is not.

Broken openings must actually open in collision/query space. A stale parent AABB
must not remain the narrowphase collider. A render crack over an intact collider
is not destruction. Conversely, no permanent family-wide collision suppression:
separated pieces must be able to collide. Former neighbours may have a short,
explicit contact-safe transition policy, not an indefinite invisibility to physics.

Map attachments, held-object hit locations and joint anchors to material part IDs
or other stable locations, not array indices that are renumbered during splitting.
They follow the owning surviving piece, release with an event, or fail explicitly.
Never duplicate attached scripts/components onto every piece by default.

Demonstrate a generic attachment or M45 joint surviving the split of its other end.
Do not reopen the already-integrated slider fixes. Material fracture does not
require automatic break thresholds on every unrelated M45 joint type.

## 15. Focused regression and final candidate gate

Develop in small proofs, then run one combined final gate. Important checks:
- Asset partition mass/coverage, adjacency, stable IDs and instance independence;
  invalid/overlapping cooks rejected; unit-scale/support assumptions explicit.
- Below-threshold survival, tension/shear failure, plastic-yield-before-fracture;
  rigid rotation and unforced freefall do not cause false failure. Compare practical
  dt/substeps/iterations and a supported-load reference such as force/area or mg.
- Alternate load path survives one broken bond; complete cut creates correct islands;
  partial crack and later progressive fracture; remaining elements retain rest state.
- Zero-impulse split preserves mass/COM/linear/angular momentum with documented error;
  no duplicated contact impulse, mass at interface nodes or unexplained energy burst.
- Current deformed shape retained, interior faces exposed, old collider removed;
  fragments collide, M44 hits current geometry and the intended opening is traversable.
- Attachment/body ownership remaps correctly; stale hits/handles fail; callback removal
  and slot reuse safe; no unrelated world-anchored joint is removed.
- Rotated uniform, radial and fixed-origin large-coordinate fixtures; no world-up rule.
- Save mid-damage/after split, fresh-process restore, unloaded-region revisit, adopted
  and explicitly removed pieces, stable first resumed steps and another break afterward.
- Piece-budget/prepare failure is atomic; offscreen simulation and multi-camera
  presentation agree; API/types/manifest/cookbook and editor/export paths work.

## Method references — scoped starting points, not another research programme

The design in this brief is a Judas integration proposal, not a claim that these
sources implement M62/M63 or certify its behaviour.

R1. NVIDIA Blast low-level API and asset/support model. Prepartitioned chunks,
bonds and connected components; the core is physics/graphics agnostic. Use the
separation of responsibilities, not its default damage shader as evidence of
physically solved structural loading. No wholesale PhysX integration is implied.
https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/api_ll_users_guide.html
https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/introduction.html

R2. Macklin, Müller, Chentanez (2016), XPBD. Constraint-force estimates can drive
force-dependent effects, including breakable constraints; finite-iteration force
error still matters. Use only if it matches the accepted M62 formulation.
https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf

R3. NVIDIA Blast stress extension. Separates tensile/compressive/shear demand and
explicit support reactions from mere support-graph connectivity. Its documentation
also distinguishes supported gravity loading from freefall. This is an optional
method reference, not authorization for a second full simulation stack.
https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/kit/docs/blast-sdk/latest/docs/api/extensions/ext_stress.html

R4. O'Brien, Bargteil, Hodgins (2002), Graphical Modeling and Animation of Ductile
Fracture. Background on coupling plastic yielding to fracture. Do not adopt its
complete offline FEM/remeshing pipeline; M63 deliberately permits baked interfaces.
https://cal.cs.umbc.edu/Papers/Obrien-2002-GMA/index.html
