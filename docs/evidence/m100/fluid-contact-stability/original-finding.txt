Confirmed current experimental-fluid coupling defect, not merely an observation
of render jitter. Matched first-step tests use the unchanged Alpha engine,
authored poses/masses, 1/60 s timestep, the full ordinary simulation path and no
player input. No solver/cadence/quality/geometry/source settings were retuned.

Controls:

  all five dynamic bodies settle. The spacecraft speed after 120 steps is tiny.
- Full scene with only advanced fluids disabled also settles normally.
- Full scene with advanced fluids enabled accelerates the spacecraft to
  17.17 m/s on step 2 and 25.4241886 m/s on step 3, throwing nearby props.

Mechanism witness before the first erroneous load:
- Minimum particle-to-ship clearance including particle radius: 1.77590406 m.
- Hydrostatic reconstruction classifies fraction=0.16711047 of ship volume wet.
- Buoyancy-force estimate: (14909.855,25487.676,-79932.25) N, approximately 85 kN.
- Drag force is only (-74.503319,-134.63582,408.68658) N; it is not the dominant kick.
- Maximum particle speed after first liquid step: 2.2781103 m/s.

FluidHydrostatics.cpp passes the whole body's bound radius to the local column
query for every volume sample. Its documented shoreline extrapolation admits
nearby water outside the actual solid's wet support. The resulting false immersed
volume is loaded with the reconstructed liquid material acceleration via
F=-rho*V*(g-a_liquid), producing this large, wrongly directed force.
ProductionFluidCoupling::PrepareRigidStep applies that force before the next rigid
solve. The full physics path then correctly responds to the supplied force.
This establishes the coupling path; it does not certify every legacy fluid case.

No production fix has been applied. Disabling advanced fluids was an independent
control, NOT a claimed fix. Basic water and swimming use a separate path.
A proper repair must constrain wet-support/extrapolation at shorelines while
retaining legitimate reconstruction around displaced solids, with dry-gap,
submerged-body and accelerating-liquid regressions. Do not hide it using damping,
force clamps, reduced cadence or changing the historical scene alone.
