# M33 — camera-to-surface rendering

**Status through M69:** M33 is operator-accepted and checkpointed. Candidate/pending
statements and measurements below record the original milestone review state,
not a current outstanding acceptance gate. Later contracts take precedence;
start with [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) and [JudasJS](JUDASJS.md).

Feature candidate; operator visual acceptance and checkpointing are pending.
No portals, recursive render passes or reflected-camera clipping are implemented.

## Ownership and path

An optional `SceneRenderCameraComponent` belongs to an ordinary stable scene
object. Its object transform defines local **-Z forward / +Y up**. Perspective
settings are vertical FOV, near/far planes, width/height (1–4096), enabled state
and an integer rendered-frame cadence. A camera may share a rigid-body object;
presentation reads that body's resolved/presented pose, not a second simulation.

`RuntimeWorld` owns transient camera presentation records and borrows the host's
Renderer, which must outlive the world, as with existing GPU-backed world data.
Enabled cameras allocate lazily on their first scheduled render. Disabled cameras
perform no render work and allocate no initial target. Disabling an already used
camera retains its last image until world destruction. Stop destroys the targets.

Renderer alone creates/deletes the framebuffer, RGBA8 colour texture and depth24
renderbuffer. Creation checks framebuffer completeness and reports failure.
Resize is transactional: failure preserves the old target; successful replacement
invalidates its old borrowed texture handle. Failed runtime allocation is reported
and retried after a resolution change or world rebuild. Opaque table entries follow
the existing append-only texture-handle convention; deleted GPU objects are not
kept alive. Raw OpenGL remains in Renderer.

`RenderWorldFrame` builds the ordinary world shadow maps once, then schedules the
secondary colour/transparency passes, then renders the unchanged main-camera
pass. All views use the same interpolation alpha, geometry, lights and RuntimeWorld.
There are no extra physics/gameplay/animation ticks. Shadow maps are shared with
the main view; this milestone does not extend their coverage or redesign shadows.
The target pass restores the draw/read framebuffer, viewport and camera matrices.

## Ordinary material consumption

`SceneRenderComponent::textureCamera` references the camera object's stable ID.
It is mutually exclusive with `textureAsset`. Box, sphere and imported-mesh
renderables resolve the generated colour through the ordinary `TextureHandle`
and lit `DrawMesh` shader. Generated textures are not disk assets and have no
AssetDatabase identity. Compound/terrain target-texture consumers are not supported.

Sampling the active target is intercepted at the actual texture binding in
Renderer and replaced with its white fallback; `feedbackFallbacks` counts this.
Disabled cameras retain their last completed image; destroyed cameras resolve to
the white fallback. References identify stable authored definitions, so consumers
may retain a reference after camera destruction without invalidating delta preflight.
Other targets may display their last completed image. Passes run in authored order;
there is no recursive traversal, dependency scheduling or portal rendering.

## Authoring and operator validation

1. Open `assets/scenes/camera_surfaces.judas` in `judas_editor`, or run it with
   `./build/judas assets/scenes/camera_surfaces.judas`.
2. Create a camera through **World → Create → Render camera**, or add its component
   through the component registry. Edit its ordinary transform and camera settings.
3. On a box, sphere or imported mesh, choose its **Camera texture** reference.
   Selecting a camera clears the disk texture selection.
4. Save/reopen, then Play. Live targets run in Play/runtime; edit mode shows the
   ordinary material fallback rather than allocating a second runtime world.
5. Stop restores authored state. Camera/material edits participate in undo/redo.

The demo uses a framed 640×360 display and a separate coloured stage. Its red
body moves through ordinary rigid velocity/contact response; the stage camera
looks away from the display, avoiding feedback in the main demonstration. An
alternate camera is authored but disabled by default.
The player/main camera is independent. No object-name logic or custom display shader
exists. The displayed image is lit by the normal material shader, not emissive.

Operator checks: confirm live subject motion on the surface, move an authored
camera and replay, change cadence, disable it, and confirm the main camera remains
independent. The automated fixture separately verifies the safe white self-feedback fallback. Screenshots are automated evidence, not human acceptance.

## Serialization and compatibility

Scene format 3 gains optional `render-camera` / `camera.*` fields and
`render.texture-camera`. Existing scenes remain readable. Invalid settings and
missing/conflicting camera references fail rather than silently selecting an asset.
Camera settings and stable material references enter canonical fingerprint
**schema 3**. Under the existing strict FTFT1 policy, schema-2 saves are rejected;
there is no silent migration, deletion or reinterpretation. Authored scenes remain
separate from runtime images, cadence counters and world deltas.

## Evidence

See [executed results](evidence/m33/RESULTS.md). Focused tests use actual EngineHost,
Renderer, RuntimeWorld, GPU readback and InteractivePlay; they do not reproduce
the renderer. The test also records synchronized CPU/render wall time and actual
draw submissions for disabled, every-frame, every-2 and every-4 scheduling.
These small-scene numbers are evidence, not GPU-only timings or a universal budget.
