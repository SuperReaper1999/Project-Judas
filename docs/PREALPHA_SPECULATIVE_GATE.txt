2. SPECULATIVE-ENCOUNTER GATE — REJECTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: a newly encountered, separated, slow box/sphere pair gets a
speculative solver row instead of an initial exact TOI query (pairs whose
motion later changes are still re-queried exactly).

First whole-workload run (fraction 0.25, angle 0.1 rad):
  severe900   mean 10.919  p95 15.442  worst 38.074  events 1,191  queries 937,574
  ordinary900 mean 10.812  p95 15.304  worst 37.913  sleeping 133/1152   <-- FAIL

Events fell 76% and severe p95 fell to 15.4 ms, but the ORDINARY scene no
longer settles: 1,019 pieces stay awake and gain energy (peak speeds near
5 m/s from rest). That breaks the plan's rejection criteria, so it was not
adopted. Root causes found with a reduced room-lattice probe:
  a. Solver: a speculative row whose approach "reaches" the gap was
     treated as a restitution bounce AT the gap (target 0 with this
     scene's restitution), stopping bodies short; corrected with a
     close-the-gap target for these rows only.
  b. Dependency blind spot: a speculative row changes velocities inside
     the velocity solve, outside the event pass; neighbours narrowphased
     at the earlier motion can then close with no constraint. Mitigated
     with a neighbour search plus a fixed 0.02 m speculative distance.
  c. Symmetry: a speculative four-point landing leaves small spurious spin
     (~2e-4 rad/s) that exact TOI landings do not; in the authored
     box-on-sphere columns (unstable equilibria that exact landings keep
     perfectly symmetric) this grows into collapse. Allowing these rows
     into normal blocks did not remove it.
After a-c the probe still did not settle. The engine's resting behaviour
currently depends on exact, symmetric TOI landings; a speculative path
would need its own landing/patch treatment, not more local patches.

6. MISS CERTIFICATES FOR NEGLIGIBLE VELOCITY CHANGES — CORRECT, NOT ADOPTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Motivation: instrumenting the "velocity changed" decision over severe900
showed 100,973 changed bodies; 28% changed by < 1 um/s and 38% by < 10 um/s
(only 5.6% were rounding-level, <= 4 ulps), yet each sent ~26 neighbours
back through CCD.
Design: a no-proximity-manifold miss at t0 proves separation > R (queried
0.1 mm beyond the reach to certify slack, without changing any answer). After
an event at `now`, a pair is skipped when
  S_old*(now - t0) + S_new*(finish - now) < R - guard
using the query's own closing-speed bound, provided each body's trajectory
is unchanged since t0 or changed once exactly at `now`.
Result (severe900, same binary):
  certificates off        mean 11.959  p95 21.580  worst 41.201  queries 489,270
  validation (query run)  zero violations; any wrong skip would have thrown
  certificates on         mean 11.836  p95 21.723  worst 39.797  queries 410,069
  digests identical to 8d87a640 on all 900 severe and ordinary steps.
16% of queries and 23% of advancement iterations were removed, but time
changed ~1% (within run-to-run noise) and the worst step did not move. Not
adopted: complexity without a material gain.
Patch: .cache/speculative-gate-20261022/rejected-miss-certificates/.
Correction to an earlier claim: the profiler's inclusive "CCD search"
scope (~10.8 ms in the worst step) overstated how much wall time the
ImpactTime advancement loop costs; removing 23% of its iterations saved ~1%.

7. SIMULTANEOUS-CONTACT INVESTIGATION (23 Oct 2026) — FINDINGS, NOT ADOPTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: why do landings need separate TOI events, and why can't the
ordinary velocity solve take those contacts together without penetration,
artificial energy or unstable stacking?
Evidence root: .cache/speculative-gate-20261022/simultaneous-investigation/
(patch simultaneous-contact.diff, test SpeculativeGateTests.cpp, probe source).

7.1 Corrections to sections 2 and 5
  * The probe test target was linked from a stale binary for part of the
    earlier work (fixed by reconfiguring). The section 5 "13-24 mm"
    dynamic-only result and the 40-iteration "bit-identical" diagnostic are
    therefore INVALID. On a fresh build the dynamic-only gate penetrates at most
    ~5 mm.
  * The ordinary "all 1,152 asleep" gate measures preservation of an
    authored, perfectly aligned lattice. With 0.5 mm horizontal jitter the
    UNMODIFIED exact engine also fails to settle: its box-on-sphere columns
    (unstable equilibria) topple, and KE rises 0.1 -> 390 J from released
    potential energy. A full energy audit (KE + rotational + PE) shows no mode
    manufactures net energy. Exact mode itself gains ~15 J between steps 30
    and 90 (position correction of 7.6 mm penetrations).

7.2 The asymmetry source, and a contract-preserving block correction
  ContactSolver::PrepareBlocks admitted a row to a normal block only when
  signedSeparation <= 0. A patch arriving exactly at contact has corners at
  +1e-7 and -1e-7, so it was split into sequential point rows. Their
  Gauss-Seidel order adds ~2e-4 rad/s of spurious spin (measured), which an
  unstable column then amplifies. Exact TOI landings end a hair inside the
  contact and always form blocks, which is why they stayed symmetric. Allowing
  anchored positive-gap rows into blocks (target -gap/dt is an ordinary LCP
  row) makes the speculative lattice settle like exact: all asleep by
  step 90, deepest 0.2 mm (exact 3.1 mm), energy constant.
  On its own (exact routing) this rule passes impact_timing, M91
  continuity and rigid_contact, and is digest-identical on ordinary900.
  But severe900 gave mean/p95/worst 12.03/23.55/42.88 ms: NO performance
  gain. Not adopted alone.

7.3 Speculative routing + gap-tolerant blocks: large gain, breaks contracts
  run          mean    p95     worst  over16.7  events  sleep  deepest  worst frame
  ordinary900  1.669   6.110   10.28     0         32  1152   0.22 mm     67.6 ms
  severe900   10.701  14.018   28.76    24        481   139   6.12 mm     75.3 ms
  live120     11.208  12.605   23.49    22        483    —    6.12 mm     60.8 ms
  (8d87a640 for comparison: severe900 11.58/20.98/39.5 ms, 112 over budget,
   5,020 events; live120 worst frame 197.8 ms, worst step 39.3 ms.)
  A negative-control family (12 fast/spinning cases at a thin wall) crosses
  in 2 cases with eligibility bypassed and in none with exact TOI or the
  default gate.
  Contract failures (fixtures not changed):
    impact_timing: isolated outgoing velocity (12), post-impact isolated floor
      event actual impact time (2) and restitution (2), restitution threshold
      suppresses micro bounce (1)
    collision_continuity (M91): new separated body receives actual collision
      impulse (2), real impact revalidates a gapped support (2)
    rigid_contact: static friction on a rotated 20-degree incline (97 mm creep)
  Restitutive encounters above 0.5 m/s were already routed back to exact TOI.
  Root cause of the impact_timing/M91 failures: a speculative row
  is a ONE-STEP-LAGGED impact. It applies only enough impulse to close the gap
  (the approacher still moves at -gap/dt and the struck body receives part of
  the momentum), with the remainder in the next step. These contracts require
  the full impulse at the actual contact time with analytical endpoints
  (2e-6). Meeting them needs contact times inside the step, which is what TOI
  events provide. The current architecture is deliberately sequential in
  that sense.

7.4 What a correct simultaneous response would require
  Either (a) accept a lagged-impact contract for slow, non-bouncing
  box/sphere encounters, with the gap-tolerant block rule, and revise the
  affected fixtures deliberately (an operator decision; not done here); or
  (b) keep exact arrival semantics but resolve each arriving patch at its own
  time inside one shared solve (time-ordered rows within a step), which is
  new work not attempted here. Neither more velocity iterations nor a full
  rewrite was indicated by the evidence.

8. Option (b) investigated: sharing work across exact-time events (2026-10-23)
  Question: within one step, do successive TOI events repeatedly do the SAME
  work, so that related contacts could be resolved together while each
  arrival keeps its exact time? Diagnostic-only instrumentation (reverted;
  nothing committed to src) on severe900 at 69b23643:

8.1 Repetition exists and is concentrated in the worst steps
  Whole run: 4,574 events in 900 steps, 395 steps with more than one event.
    Domain bodies 147,987 vs 104,462 unique per step (1.42x).
    Changed bodies 100,973 vs 74,132 unique (1.36x).
  Worst steps: re-solving of 2-3.3x, e.g.
    step 258, 39.7 ms: 28 events, 1,711 domain / 522 unique;
    step 314, 38.7 ms: 17 events, 1,745 / 607.
  Same-instant events already share one dependency flush (existing
  exact-equality coalescing in AdvanceImpacts).

8.2 Cost of the per-event parts (main-thread PC samples by phase, worst 40 steps)
    dependency flush + TOI queries   19.1%
    domain expansion geometry         9.8%  (PairAt/candidates in TryIncrementalImpact)
    growing solve arithmetic          9.0%
    publish/invalidate                7.1%
    -> 45% of worst-step time. Ordinary narrowphase 12.7%, velocity solve 12.6%.
  Over the whole burst these per-event parts are only about 17%; the
  velocity solve (25.6%) and narrowphase (23.5%) dominate.
  Upper bound: removing ALL cross-event repetition (domains 2.4x -> 1x)
  would save at most about 26% of a worst step (about 40 -> 30 ms), still
  far above 16.7 ms, and only by merging distinct arrival times.

8.3 The repeated work is not the same work
  Of 447,004 domain PairAt evaluations, 139,187 (31%) revisit a pair already
  evaluated earlier in the same step. Only 147 (0.03%) of those see
  bit-identical poses for both bodies. The rest evaluate a genuinely changed
  state: a later time, and velocities changed by the intervening event.
  Under exact arrival times each event must solve its own domain at its own
  instant. No exact cache, shared domain or shared solve can reuse the
  earlier result, and the module's "no cross-time contact truth" rule is
  correct here. Exact sharing would gain about 0%.

8.4 Decision
  Not prototyped further, per the operator's instruction not to force a
  change without meaningful gain. The material reduction in the measured
  record still comes only from reducing the NUMBER of exact-time events, that
  is, a lagged/speculative contract for slow, non-bouncing encounters
  (section 7.3, an operator decision on impact_timing/M91/rigid_contact).
  Or the bounded remaining hitch (worst step about 40 ms) is accepted under
  exact semantics. No human validation is claimed.
